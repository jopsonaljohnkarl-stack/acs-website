Where offshore freelancers work, and where they do not

We are not going to tell you freelancers are useless. For a defined project with a clear end point, a good freelancer is often the right answer. The difficulty is ongoing operational work. That is where the model strains.

  • They have other clients, so your urgent job queues behind someone else's
  • Screening is whatever you have time to do yourself
  • If they stop responding, you start the search again from nothing
  • Your process lives in their head, and it leaves with them

For a one off task that is a fair trade. For the work that keeps your business running, it is a risk you carry alone.

Where traditional outsourcing falls short

A BPO solves the continuity problem. There is a process behind the person and someone else manages it. What you often give up is control and visibility.

  • The work runs to the provider's process, not yours
  • You may not know who is doing your work, or how many people it passes through
  • Problems go into a queue rather than to a person
  • Scope changes become a negotiation

For high volume, standardised work that suits many businesses at once. For work that has to run your way, it often does not.

A managed service costs more on day one and removes more risk

The candidate is screened and assessed against a role designed around your operation. You choose from a shortlist with scorecards rather than a marketplace listing. Administration and payments are handled by our staffing provider, so that work does not land on you. And ACS stays involved after the person starts, measuring the work against what was agreed.

The honest trade is this. A managed service is not the cheapest option on day one. What it removes is the management load and the risk of starting over.
