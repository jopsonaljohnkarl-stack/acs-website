Distance creates a real risk of drift, without active management

The most common failure in offshore arrangements is not a bad hire. It is a good hire left unmanaged, where standards slowly drift because nobody is actively watching performance against what was originally agreed.

  • Nobody is reviewing output regularly, so problems are found late, if at all
  • There is no defined measure of what good performance actually looks like
  • Small issues go unaddressed and slowly compound into bigger ones
  • The client has no visibility beyond assuming the work is getting done

This is precisely the gap active performance management closes, and it is why ACS treats it as an ongoing responsibility rather than a one time setup task. A role that is placed well and then left alone tends to drift for exactly the same reason any unmanaged process drifts: nobody is actively checking it against the standard originally agreed.

ACS owns performance oversight. Our delivery partner owns employment.

This split matters because it means someone is actively responsible for whether the work is good, separate from who administers the employment relationship.

A conversation about whether a report was accurate should never be confused with a conversation about someone's leave entitlements, and keeping the two apart is what makes performance management genuinely clear.

Performance measures are set before the role starts, not invented afterward

Generic measures do not work, because a telesales role and an operations manager role succeed in genuinely different ways. Measures are agreed during scoping, specific to what the role actually does.

  • Output measures relevant to the role, such as calls made, invoices processed, or tickets resolved
  • Quality measures, such as accuracy rates or client feedback where relevant
  • Responsiveness measures, such as turnaround time on requests
  • Any specific measures you nominate that matter to your business particularly

These measures become the baseline established during the first 90 days, referenced in every reporting cycle afterward. Setting them before the role starts matters, since agreeing what good looks like after the fact tends to produce a moving target rather than a fair standard.

Regular reporting, not just a review when something goes wrong

Reporting happens on an agreed cadence, typically covering output against the measures set during scoping, any issues identified, and any changes worth discussing.

  • A regular reporting cycle, frequency agreed during scoping and typically weekly or fortnightly to start
  • Reports built around the specific measures relevant to the role, not a generic template
  • A standing check in with your named ACS contact, not just a document sent with no conversation
  • An open channel for you to raise concerns at any point, not only at the scheduled review

Issues are addressed directly, not left to resolve themselves

Performance dips happen, for entirely normal reasons: a role that was scoped too broadly, a skill gap that only became visible once real work started, or simply an off period. What matters is what happens next.

ACS raises performance concerns with you directly, discusses the likely cause, and agrees a path forward, whether that is additional support, a scope adjustment, or in some cases a conversation about whether the role or the person is the right fit.

This is not left until the next scheduled report. Genuine issues are raised as soon as they are identified, since waiting only makes them more expensive to fix. In most cases the fix is straightforward, additional guidance, a clarified expectation, or a small scope adjustment, rather than anything drastic.

Where a genuine mismatch is identified between the person and the role, that is addressed under the replacement terms in the service agreement, rather than left to linger simply because starting over feels like a hassle. Acting on it early is almost always cheaper than waiting to see if the problem resolves itself, which it rarely does.

Common questions

Can I request changes to how performance is measured?

Yes. Measures are reviewed periodically and can be adjusted as the role evolves or as you learn more about what actually matters for your business.

Do I get reports even if everything is going well?

Yes. Reporting is a standing part of the engagement, not something that only appears when there is a problem to explain.

What if I want more frequent updates than the standard cadence?

That can be arranged during scoping or adjusted later. The standard cadence is a starting point, not a fixed limit.

Who do I talk to if I am not satisfied with the reporting itself?

Your named ACS contact. Reporting format and frequency are meant to work for you, not just to satisfy an internal process.

Is performance data shared with the delivery partner?

Performance oversight sits with ACS. Employment related matters are shared with the delivery partner only where relevant to their role in supporting the team member.
