How offshore staffing works, from first call to first day

Here is how offshore staffing works with ACS, in five steps. You are involved in three of them, and here is what each one takes from you.

The five steps

Step 1. ACS scopes the role around your operation

A conversation about what the work actually is. Not a job title, the real tasks and the systems they sit in. We will also tell you if part of the work should stay in Australia. Some of it usually should.

Your time: about an hour. Owner: ACS.

Step 2. Our provider sources and screens against that scope

Our established offshore staffing provider sources candidates and puts them through skills testing, English assessment and role specific assessments.

Your time: none. Owner: staffing provider.

Step 3. You review scorecards, interview and choose

You receive a shortlist with scorecards, so you can compare candidates on the same basis rather than on impressions. You interview and you decide. Nobody is allocated to you. If nobody is right, we go back and source again.

Your time: an hour or two of interviews. Owner: you, supported by ACS.

Step 4. Onboarding runs, and ACS integrates the role

Our provider handles onboarding, payments and administrative setup. ACS handles the part that decides whether this works: system access, introductions, a documented process, and making sure your team knows how the role fits.

Your time: a few hours across the first week, mostly showing them how you like things done. Owner: shared.

Step 5. ACS provides ongoing service oversight

We measure the work against the performance expectations agreed in step one, keep communication running, and stay involved in the service. Administrative support continues through our provider in the background.

Your time: ongoing, the same as any team member. Usually a short weekly catch up. Owner: ACS.

Remote, to agreed Australian client hours

Team members work remotely using business-grade equipment and connectivity, with working hours aligned to agreed Australian client hours. Operational support is provided through established remote management processes.

Ask for a realistic timeframe on the first call

The honest answer depends on the role and how specific the requirements are. Specialist roles take longer than general administration. We will give you a realistic timeframe for your role on the first call, rather than a number that suits our marketing.

Four things that decide whether this works

  • Be specific about the work in step one. Vague scoping produces the wrong shortlist.
  • Make time for the interviews.
  • Give access to the systems the role needs.
  • Treat them as part of the team. The clients who get the most out of this are the ones who do.
