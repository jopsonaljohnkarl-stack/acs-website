Coordination work fills the day without ever feeling finished

Operations admin rarely looks urgent in the moment, which is exactly why it piles up until it becomes a genuine problem.

  • Scheduling changes and clashes are managed reactively, discovered only when something is double booked
  • Day to day coordination between people, jobs and suppliers takes constant small interruptions
  • Nobody owns the operational admin properly, so it falls to whoever has a spare moment
  • Small tasks pile up into a backlog that eats a full day to clear

This usually shows up as a feeling rather than a single event: the sense that the day disappeared into logistics rather than anything that actually grew the business. That is the operations assistant gap.

Coordination runs on information, not physical presence

Scheduling, calendar management and coordination between people all run through shared systems: calendars, messaging tools, spreadsheets. None of it requires the assistant to be in the same building as the people being coordinated.

What it requires is consistency and attention to detail, checking that a change in one place is reflected everywhere else. A dedicated team member working set hours can maintain that consistency far more reliably than an owner fitting it in between other tasks.

It also creates a single source of truth. When one person owns the coordination, information stops living in someone's head and starts living in a system everyone can check.

Scales with your team, not against it

Most businesses start with scheduling, since it has the fastest visible effect. Broader coordination and admin support is typically added once the scheduling process is running smoothly.

As your team grows, the same operations assistant can generally absorb more coordination before a second is needed, since the process does not change, only the number of people and tasks it covers.

Some businesses eventually add a second assistant to split scheduling from broader admin, once both areas are busy enough on their own to justify a dedicated person each.

Common questions

Can this role use our specific project management or scheduling tool?

Yes, once trained on it during onboarding. Screening is done against the specific tools your business runs.

Is this different from an admin assistant role?

Yes. Operations assistants focus on scheduling and coordination between people and tasks. Admin assistants focus more on documents, email and customer facing admin. Many businesses use both.

Can the role expand to cover supplier coordination?

Yes, that is a common addition once the core scheduling and internal coordination process is running well.

How much oversight does this role need from me?

Most of the day to day runs independently once the role is established. You stay involved in exceptions and anything that needs a judgement call.

Does this suit a business with a small team?

Yes. Smaller teams often benefit the most, since there is less existing capacity to absorb coordination work without dedicated support.
