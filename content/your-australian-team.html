A name and a face, not a ticket number

One of the most common frustrations with offshore staffing done badly is losing track of who is actually accountable once a placement is made. The team member starts, and from that point on every query goes into a queue, gets picked up by whoever is available, and has to be re-explained from scratch each time.

ACS is structured specifically to avoid that. You are assigned a named contact who understands your business, stays with you through scoping and into ongoing performance oversight, and is the person you call when something needs attention, not a rotating roster of whoever picked up the ticket.

This matters more than it might seem on paper. A named contact who already knows your business can act on a concern in minutes. A support queue has to rebuild that context every single time, which is exactly the kind of friction that makes offshore engagements quietly underperform.

The roles involved in your engagement, from scoping onward

Named profiles for each of these roles are added as they are approved. The structure below reflects how the team is organised regardless of which specific people are publicly named at any given time.

Your scoping contact

The person who runs the initial conversation about what the role actually is, translating your workflow into a scope our offshore staffing provider can source and screen against. This is the person asking detailed questions about your business, not reading from a generic script.

Your account contact

Your ongoing point of contact once the engagement is live. This is who you raise concerns with, who coordinates performance reviews, and who you can reach directly rather than through a general enquiry line.

Performance oversight

The function responsible for reviewing reported performance against the measures agreed during scoping, and for escalating anything that needs your input. On smaller engagements this may be the same person as your account contact. On larger ones it may be a separate function working alongside them.

Leadership

The people ultimately accountable for the business relationship, available if an issue needs to be raised beyond your day to day contact. This escalation path exists but is rarely needed, since most matters are resolved at the account contact level.

Continuity is designed in, not left to chance

Many offshore staffing providers assign a salesperson to close the deal and a different, often rotating, support function to manage the relationship afterward. The person who understood your business during scoping is not the person you deal with once the engagement is live.

ACS keeps the same named contact structure running from the first scoping call through ongoing performance oversight. The person who helped design your role is either the same person managing it afterward, or someone who was directly involved and briefed, not someone reading your file for the first time when you call with a concern.

The handover between roles is deliberate, not accidental

Your scoping contact does not simply hand off a brief and disappear. In most engagements, they remain involved through the first 90 days, briefing your account contact in detail on what was agreed and why, so nothing gets lost in translation between the person who designed the role and the person managing it afterward.

As the engagement matures, your account contact becomes the primary relationship, drawing on performance oversight input to keep reporting accurate and to flag anything worth discussing before it becomes a bigger issue. Leadership stays informed at a summary level without needing to be involved in day to day detail, which is exactly how an escalation path should work: present, but not intrusive.

This structure exists because we have seen what happens when it is missing. A scoping conversation that never gets passed on properly leads to a team member being managed against expectations nobody explicitly agreed to, which is a completely avoidable source of friction.

Why this page does not yet show individual profiles

Publishing someone's name, photograph and biography publicly is a decision that belongs to that person, not just to the business. Each profile on this page is added only once the individual has approved being featured, which is a standing rule applied consistently across the site.

This means the page may launch with role descriptions only, filling in named profiles progressively rather than delaying the whole page until every approval is in. We think an honest structure without names is a better outcome than an empty page or, worse, placeholder names that do not represent real people.

Common questions

Will I deal with the same person throughout the engagement?

Your account contact is intended to be consistent for the life of the engagement. Team changes happen occasionally, as in any business, but continuity is the standing goal, not an exception.

Can I meet my contact before committing to an engagement?

Yes. Your scoping contact is who you speak with on the initial call, and in most cases continues to be involved afterward.

What if I am not satisfied with my account contact?

Raise it with them directly, or escalate to leadership if needed. This is a genuinely rare situation, but the escalation path exists and is not a hollow gesture.

Do I ever deal directly with the offshore delivery partner?

No. ACS is your point of contact throughout. The delivery partner handles employment administration in the background, not client facing communication.

Is the Australian team involved in day to day task supervision?

Day to day task direction typically comes from you, since the team member works on your business specifically. Your ACS contact is involved in performance oversight and escalation, not micromanaging daily tasks.
